### Abstract

Network Science and Deep Graph Learning can help us to Understand AI Swarms

AI swarms are networks at two scales. Inside each agent, a large language model is a computation graph whose reasoning is carried by sparse sub-networks, so-called circuits. Between agents, collective behavior emerges from who interacts with whom, and when. In this input talk, I argue that network science and deep graph learning provide a common methodological lens for both scales. First, I show how the task of localizing circuits in transformer models can be framed as a graph learning problem, in which a single graph neural network trained across many models and tasks predicts which computational pathways matter in models it has never seen. Second, I draw on our research on collaboration networks in open source software teams to ask what it can teach us about coordination in AI swarms: How are the topology and temporal dynamics of interactions related to collective performance? How do diversity of expertise and the division of roles shape team success? And where does the analogy break down, e.g. because individual motivation, a key driver of human teams, has no obvious counterpart in AI agents. I close with open questions on how time-aware graph learning can help to monitor, understand, and steer coordination in (potentially malicious) AI swarms.
