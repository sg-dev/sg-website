### Abstract

Can Swarm Intelligence Make AI Collectives Predictable?

Classical swarm-intelligence algorithms rely on simple,
reactive agents that use local information and decentralized
communication, enabling scalability and robustness. 
A central challenge is the micro-macro link: predicting
global behavior from local rules and designing local rules
that produce desired collective outcomes.

AI swarms differ because their agents have higher cognitive
capabilities and often share global information. It remains
unclear whether greater decentralization would improve these
systems, or whether swarm-intelligence models can help
predict their behavior. 

Collective decision-making mechanisms such as cross-inhibition, which suppresses competing alternatives and can prevent deadlocks, offer concrete ideas to test. Similar mechanisms of structured criticism and counter-evidence might help AI swarms avoid premature consensus and correlated errors.
