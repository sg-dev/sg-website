### Abstract


As AI systems become more agentic, what should it mean to understand them?

A common desideratum for explanations is faithfulness: an explanation should accurately reflect how a system operates. But faithfulness alone is not enough. An explanation can faithfully identify individual tokens, activations, model components, messages, or execution steps while providing little useful or human-understable insight into why an agent behaves as it does.

I argue that explainability for agentic systems increasingly requires choosing the right granularity, vocabulary, and level of abstraction. Attribution to individual inputs, model components, or execution events must be complemented by higher-level characterizations of system-level design or behavior.

This also changes how explanations should be evaluated. Evaluation should test whether explanations actually can help humans form useful and reusable models of a system, while separating understanding of the AI system from understanding of the task itself. Moreover, we should ask not only whether an explanation is valid in a particular setting, but also when it ceases to be valid under context or domain shift.

I will discuss why abstraction and evaluation become increasingly important as AI systems grow more complex and versatile, and what this may require from the next generation of explainability methods.
